# Proposed adjacent-line validation — fictional plan

The project team proposes an **eight-week** validation on one adjacent flexible line. Approval would authorize a limited test and a later go/no-go review, not a site-wide deployment. The shadow would remain advisory and read-only.

1. **Weeks 1–2 — connect and qualify.** Confirm line interfaces, station ownership, recipe identifiers, timestamp quality, and cybersecurity review. Name operations and engineering owners.
2. **Weeks 3–4 — establish the baseline.** Collect day- and night-shift data, reconcile scheduled hours and downtime definitions, and test buffering/replay during planned network interruption.
3. **Weeks 5–6 — operator validation.** Review flag relevance and duplicate rates with operators; refine advisory wording, recipe context, and shift filters. Record missed events when a defensible label can be established.
4. **Weeks 7–8 — compare and decide.** Repeat the agreed measures, document data gaps, and return to operations, safety, IT, and engineering for a go/no-go decision.

Proposed gates include at least **98% timestamp completeness** in the adjacent-line validation dataset, documented review of day/night shift performance, an agreed method for labelling missed events, and sign-off on access, retention, and control boundaries. The 98% threshold is a **future acceptance target**, not a measured pilot result. No expansion should be implied if these gates are not met.
